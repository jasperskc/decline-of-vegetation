The issue

Human activity is driving vegetation decline

South Auckland is experiencing a serious decline in vegetation as more land is used for housing, roads, infrastructure, and farming to accomodate the rising population.

What's happening?

As Auckland's population grows, forests, wetlands, coastal ecosystems, and native habitats are being placed under increasing pressure. South Auckland has been especially affected because its flat land is suitable for development and sits close to major transport and growth corridors.

Between 1996 and 2018, Auckland's urban area expanded by around 30,264 hectares, with much of this growth occurring in southern and outer areas. That growth has created new housing and infrastructure, but it has also reduced and fragmented natural vegetation.

Wetlands have been hit particularly hard. Around 90% of Auckland's original wetland vegetation has been lost, mainly because land has been drained for farming and urban development.
