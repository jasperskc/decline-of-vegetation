Species Planted

Carex

Pūrei Riparian restoration & water filtering

A native sedge that thrives in wet conditions near waterways. Supports riparian restoration by filtering sediment from runoff.

Apodasmia similis

Oioi Coastal margin & salt tolerance

An endemic New Zealand reed commonly found in coastal areas and estuaries. Highly salt-tolerant and ideal for coastal restoration.

Muehlenbeckia

Wire Vine Ground cover & erosion control

A native climbing plant that forms dense ground cover, helping to stabilise soil and prevent erosion. Provides habitat for small insects and lizards.

Interview with Park Ranger

Auckland Council Parks & Community Facilities

Q: Why was this site chosen for restoration work and how does it benefit the environment?

Park Ranger It provides an opportunity to increase native habitat for fauna in an area. The restoration work helps create shelter and food sources for wildlife, improving local biodiversity. As the plants grow, they also absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and release oxygen.

Q: What environmental issues are affecting this area most?

Park Ranger One of the main environmental issues affecting this area is the presence of pests both plants and animals. Invasive weeds can outcompete native plants for sunlight, water, and nutrients, while pest species can damage vegetation and disrupt local ecosystems.

Q: Has human caused urban development impacted this area? How?

Park Ranger Yes. Land clearance has reduced native vegetation.

Q: What native species are being planted today, and why were they selected?

Park Ranger We planted carex plants, oioi, and muehlenbeckia. These species were selected because they naturally occur in this area and are well adapted to the local conditions.

Q: How does volunteering at events like this create both immediate and long-term benefits to the surrounding environments?

Park Ranger In the short term, volunteering at events like this helps create a stronger connection between people and nature. And in the long term, volunteers help plant native species and restore areas where the environment has declined. These efforts contribute to healthier ecosystems, increased biodiversity, and improved habitat for native wildlife.

Q: How do you measure whether a planting project has been successful. Are there any tests you do?

Park Ranger One way we measure success is by ensuring that plants are correctly planted so they are not easily dislodged. We do not carry out tests, but we monitor the site and maintain the plants for around two years after planting. This includes controlling weeds with herbicides.
